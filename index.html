02 Projects

Palomares Lab · with Dr. Emily Schwitzgebel

How source labels shape music judgments

A study of how identifying music as human-created or AI-generated affects listeners’ evaluations.

First-author manuscript · under review at PLOS ONE

The questions
Can listeners distinguish human-created songs from AI-generated versions designed to match them? How does revealing the source affect their evaluations?
My contribution
  • Co-led the listener study and am first author on the manuscript.
  • Developed the Python pipeline that generates the prompts for the AI-generated versions.
  • Lead stimulus preparation and develop the Qualtrics experiment for a follow-up study.
Approach
  1. Human-created songs
  2. AI-generated versionsdesigned to match; generated in Suno from pipeline prompts
  3. Listener studysource hidden or disclosed
  4. Compare evaluationssource-identification accuracy; listener preferences

Plain language

Listeners evaluated human-created songs and AI-generated versions designed to share selected musical features. The study compared their evaluations with the source hidden and with the source disclosed.

Scientific detail

For each human-created song, my pipeline extracted acoustic and lyric features and converted them into text prompts; Suno, an AI music generator, produced a version from each prompt.

The follow-up study tests a hypothesis: that genre prototypicality (how closely a song resembles a typical example of its genre) helps explain how AI-generated songs perform when listeners don’t know the source.

What we found

Within this study’s sample and song set, listeners did not reliably distinguish the human-created songs from their AI-generated counterparts. They preferred the AI-generated songs when the source was hidden and the human-created songs when the source was disclosed.

Why it interests me

This work made me more interested in the expectations and experiences people bring to a judgment. That interest is also part of why I’m drawn to medicine: I want to understand both the biology of illness and the person experiencing it—their concerns, beliefs, and what matters to them.
