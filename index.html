02 Projects

Palomares Lab · with Dr. Emily Schwitzgebel

Expectations, context, and human judgment

A study of how source labels influence responses to AI-generated music.

First-author manuscript under review at PLOS ONE · not yet published

The question
Could listeners tell human songs apart from matched AI-generated versions, and how did knowing a song’s source affect their evaluations?
My contribution
  • Built the Python pipeline that writes the prompts for the matched AI versions (next project).
  • Co-led the listener study and am first author on the manuscript.
  • For a follow-up study: lead stimulus preparation, the Qualtrics build, and comparisons involving Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini.
Approach
  1. Human songs
  2. Matched AI versionsgenerated in Suno from pipeline prompts
  3. Listener studysource hidden or labeled
  4. Compare judgmentstelling them apart; preference

Plain language

Listeners judged real songs and AI versions made to match them. Sometimes they knew which was which, and sometimes they didn’t.

Scientific detail

For each human song, my pipeline extracted acoustic and lyric features and turned them into prompts; a matched AI version was generated from those prompts in Suno. We compared listeners’ judgments when the source was hidden and when it was labeled.

The follow-up tests whether genre prototypicality helps explain AI music’s performance in blind comparisons. That is a hypothesis under investigation.

What we found

Listeners couldn’t distinguish the human songs from their AI-generated counterparts. They preferred the AI songs when the source was hidden, and the human songs when the source was labeled.

These results describe this study, not listeners or music in general.

Why it interests me

This work made me more interested in the expectations and experiences people bring to a judgment. That interest is also part of why I’m drawn to medicine: I want to understand both the biology of illness and the person experiencing it—their concerns, beliefs, and what matters to them.
